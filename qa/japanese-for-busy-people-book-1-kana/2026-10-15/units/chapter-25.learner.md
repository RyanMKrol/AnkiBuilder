# Casual Style 1 (chapter-25 + chapter-25-extras): learner pass

This chapter was pleasant to study on Recognition and harder on Production. The "casual style" hint
on every front works: I always knew I was being asked for the casual form, and it kept these cards
apart from the polite ones I learned in Lessons 10 and 11 (おおきくないです, よかったです, どうでしたか).
The adjective paradigms (おおきい, いい, きれい) are laid out cleanly in the base unit and then used in
short sentences in the extras, and the notes on dropping は, です and か are clear and useful. The
problems come from choices the chapter teaches but the fronts never ask for. There are three casual
words for "I" (ぼく, おれ, あたし), two ways to mark a casual topic (drop は, or use って), and three name
suffixes. A Production front that only says "male speaker", or says nothing about the speaker, cannot
tell me which one it wants. Two sentences also use a verb's plain negative (いかない, わからない),
which no card teaches.

## Strong spots

- The adjective forms come in sets of three with notes that explain the irregular one: `adj-ii-present-neg`,
  `adj-ii-past-aff` and `adj-ii-past-neg` say why いい becomes よ-, and the extras repeat that in
  `ii-present-neg`, `ii-past-aff` and `ii-past-neg`.
- The notes on what casual speech drops are exactly what I needed: `casual-statement-tanaka-nihonjin`
  (です drops, だ comes back before ね/よ), `heya-kirei-casual`, `kaban-ookii-casual`,
  `going-to-party-tomorrow` (no か), `tomodachi-to-iku-casual` (と stays), `doko-ni-iru-casual` (に stays).
- Scenes that do their job: `casual-answer-no-nationality`, `casual-answer-yes-nationality`,
  `yeah-im-going`, `food-was-good`.
- `kirei-dane-2` and `kirei-dayo-2` are a clear minimal pair for だね against だよ.
- `atashi-2` ("spoken by a woman, casual") shows how a speaker hint should look.

## Weak spots

**unanswerable** (should-fix unless noted)

- Pronoun choice not cued: `ore-2` and `ore-3` say only "male speaker", which fits ぼく too; `boku-3`
  says "asked by a man", which fits おれ; `abe-not-mine` (おれ) and `yes-its-mine` (ぼく) have the same
  classmate in the same scene with different pronouns; `boku-sensei-janai` and `atashi-gakusei-dayo`
  have no speaker cue at all.
- って versus dropping は not cued: `nikko-tte-donna-tokoro`, `casual-question-tanaka-nihonjin-tte`,
  `is-this-yours-raja`.
- Name suffix not cued: `suffix-chan-usage` and `suffix-kun-usage` drop the suffix from the English
  and give no clue who the person is.
- `party-was-lively` (polish): "very" could be とても as well as すごく.

**ambiguous** (should-fix)

- `saa` ("Well... / I don't know.") collides with `saa-1` ("Well... I don't know.") and with
  chapter-2-extras `saa-wakarimasen`.
- `dane` and `dayo`: ね and よ alone, which I already know, also answer these fronts, and this unit's
  own notes say an い-adjective takes them directly.

**untaught-prerequisite** (should-fix)

- `saa-1` (わからない) and `ore-2` (いかない) use a verb's ない form, which no card teaches.

**leaks-answer** (polish)

- `na-adjective-casual-ne-kirei` and `na-adjective-casual-yo-kirei` print the rule being tested
  ("な-adjective + だ before casual ね") on the Production front.

**note-unverifiable** (polish)

- `tte-casual-topic` calls って a "contraction" of は, which doesn't make sense to me since they share
  no sound.

**other** (polish)

- `nikko-kirei-janakatta`'s note points to にっこう、きれいだね, which is not in the deck.
- `uchi-ookikunakatta`'s note describes how the deck was built ("already-used").
- Romaji differs between the units: ううん is "uun" in the base and "ūn" in the extras; "yokunai" and
  "yoku nai"; "Nikkō" capitalized but "hakone" lower case.
