# Lesson 13: Asking for a Place: Where Is It? (learner)

This chapter was mostly pleasant to study. The new nouns (bookstore, bakery, police box, the stationery set, the three のりば words) are easy, each counter question has a worked example and an answer card with a scene, and the ここ／そこ／あそこ trio finally gets the location words that Lesson 5 only gave me in polite form. The trouble is the chapter's main grammar point. I learn that "the restaurant is on the first floor" can end in に あります or contract to です. The grammar2 pair and the two sobaichi cards have hints that say which form the card wants. About twenty other location cards in the two units have no such hint, so on Production I am guessing between two correct answers and marking myself wrong half the time. "Where is the boat dock?" and "Where is the bookstore?" sit side by side wanting different endings. Outside that pattern there are a few real collisions (それから against も, こ against こども), one English line that breaks the chapter's own は/が rule, and some polish on notes and romaji.

## Strong spots

- The ここ／そこ／あそこ minimal triple, with the scissors (chapter-13-extras hasami-koko, hasami-soko, hasami-asoko). The English ("here next to me", "right there next to you", "over there, away from both of us") makes each one answerable. koko-ni-imasu-a and tanaka-soko-imasu-q then show the same spot being そこ to one speaker and ここ to the other, which I would not have worked out alone.
- asoko and soko carry "everyday form", which separates them cleanly from あちら and そちら (both "polite form" in Lessons 5 and 6).
- The は/が minimal pairs where the English follows the rule: ga-kouban-eki-mae / wa-kouban-eki-mae, grammar2-1 / grammar2-2, ga-ramenya-chikaku / mo-ramenya-chikaku, and the sobaichi pair with scenes (td-sobaichi-ga-oishii / wa-sobaichi-oishii).
- The ちかい／ちかく split is handled well. Every ちかい／とおい sentence has the hint "near is an adjective here ... ends in です", and every ちかく sentence has "near is the noun for the area near ... takes にあります" (tooi-example, ks5-house-near, no-hotel-chikaku-byouin-q, kara-yuubinkyoku-kaisha-q with its note on why English has no "from").
- The counter questions: ikutsu, nanbon, nanmai each have a word card, a question and an answer with a scene (apple-count-q/a, fib-fork-table-count-q/a, ex-plates-table-count-q/a, ex-cups-table-count-q/a).
- The four-way existence tense sets (kagi-drawer-arimasu ... arimasendeshita, smith-office-imasu ... imasendeshita). The note telling ありませんでした apart from じゃありませんでした is a good one.
- goro has a note that teaches the ごろ/ぐらい difference, so goro-example and sp-nanji-goro-kaerimasu-q make sense.
