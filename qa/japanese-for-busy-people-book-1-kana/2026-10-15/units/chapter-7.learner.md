# Lesson 7: Going Places (2): I'm Going by Shinkansen (learner pass)

Units: `chapter-7` (102 cards) and `chapter-7-extras` (83 cards).

This chapter is a big one, and most of it is calendar: weekdays, all twelve months and all 31 dates, plus
transport words, the means particle で and the time particle に. Studied cold, the vocabulary half
works very well. Every date card has an unambiguous "(of the month)" gloss, the irregular dates carry
short notes, and the five dates that could be misread as something else (よっか, ふつか, みっか, いつか,
むいか) get a "a date in the month" hint on the Recognition side. The extras unit drills the new に
rule against the Lesson 6 rule (relative time words take no particle) over and over, and that contrast
lands. The trouble spots are a "how" pair the deck itself calls interchangeable, a few notes that are
wrong or talk about cards I cannot see, and one hint that mislabels the destination に.

## Strong spots

- The two に jobs are kept apart well. `chapter-7/ni-particle-time` names the difference, and
  `kinyoubi-ni-tokyo-kaerimasu`, `w2-paku-mokuyoubi-kimasu` and `w2-paku-rokugatsu-katou-to-hokkaido`
  show both in one sentence with a note on each.
- Relative and absolute time are contrasted on real sentences: `smith-ototoi-basu-de-depaato`,
  `w2-raja-kyonen-indo-kaerimashita` (with its pointer to きょねんの9がつに), `w2-smith-kinou-kaerimashita`,
  `w2-natsuyasumi-ni-hokkaido-e`.
- The あるいて exception is taught cleanly on `chapter-7/aruite` and then used on
  `smith-aruite-uchi-ni-kaerimashita` and `p102-jimu-ni-aruite-ikimasu`.
- Paired Production fronts that would otherwise collide are told apart by hints: the に/へ pair
  (`w2-smith-tokyo-eki-ni-ikimasu` / `w2-smith-tokyo-eki-e-ikimasu`) and the は/も pair
  (`w2-katou-wa-shinkansen-de` / `w2-katou-mo-shinkansen-de`).
- Answer cards carry scenes, so short replies like `w2-mikka-desu`, `kugatsu-desu` and
  `fib-nichiyoubi-a` are answerable on their own.
- Irregular date notes (`hatsuka-20th`, `juuyokka-14th`, `w2-purezen-raigetsu-juuyokka`) are short and useful.

## Weak spots

**unanswerable (6, should-fix).** なんで and どうやって both mean "how (by what means)", and
`chapter-7-extras/how` says they are interchangeable, with どうやって as "the everyday choice". Every
"How ...?" Production front then has two right answers and no cue: `chapter-7/nande-ikimasuka`,
`smith-nande-osaka-ni-ikimasu-ka`, `fib-tanaka-nande-kaigi-q`, `w2-katou-nande-kaisha-ni-kimasu-ka`,
`p102-douyatte-jimu-ni-ikimasu-ka`, `p102-douyatte-shigoto-ni-ikimasu-ka`. A hint naming the word
would fix all six.

**ambiguous (2).** `chapter-7/nannichi` is glossed "What day", which in English usually asks for the
weekday (`nanyoubi`). Should-fix. `w2-sanban-no-basu-ga-ikimasu` has the same target as
`chapter-6-extras/fib-sanban-no-basu-ikimasu`, so I study the same sentence twice. Polish.

**wrong-content (4).** `tsuitachi-1st` says dates from the 11th on are regular, but this chapter teaches
the 14th, 20th and 24th as irregular (should-fix). `nichi-suffix` makes the same overgeneralization
(polish). `w2-smith-tokyo-eki-ni-ikimasu` glosses the destination に as "in/on/at", which is the time
に's gloss (should-fix). `w2-paku-mokuyoubi-kimasen` says きません is on no other card, but I studied
it in Lesson 6 extras (should-fix).

**missing-info (2, polish).** `raishuu-no-getsuyoubi-ni-sochira` drops the "(where you are)" that
told me そちら, not あちら. `juuji-ni-ikimasu` answers "what time will you come?" with いきます and
never says why.

**leaks-answer (1, polish).** `yasumi-vacation`: the Recognition hint "a longer stretch away from
work" is a definition of the answer.

**other (8, polish).** Six extras notes talk about "Member 1/2/3 of the set", which means nothing on a
card met alone in a shuffle: `w2-smith-doyoubi-kaerimasu`, `w2-smith-doyoubi-kaerimasen`,
`w2-smith-doyoubi-kaerimasu-ka`, `w2-paku-mokuyoubi-kimasu`, `w2-paku-mokuyoubi-kimasen`,
`w2-paku-mokuyoubi-kimasu-ka` (which also mentions "the exercises"). `gatsu-suffix` says this chapter
spells months in hiragana while its own cards write 4がつ. `fib-smith-taxi-kaerimashita` names a
"まし past-tense pattern" where I was taught ました.

Preflight already lists the romaji style items, the long Production faces, the note-claim INFO lines
and the "goes there" near-sibling frame, so none of those are repeated here.
