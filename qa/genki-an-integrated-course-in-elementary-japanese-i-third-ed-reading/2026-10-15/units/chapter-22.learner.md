# Chapter 23: Reading and Writing 9: Sora's Diary (chapter-22), Learner

This was an easy, pleasant chapter to study. Eight new kanji (刺, 少, 辞, 午, 情, 梅, 友, 鳥) each come with a meaning card, and then the words build on them in ways I could follow from what I already knew: 少 gives 少し, 少ない, 少年, 少女 and 少々; 友 gives 友だち, 友人, 親友 and 友情; 午 gives 午前, 午後, 午前中 and 正午. The rest are mostly compounds of kanji I already had (白紙, 白鳥, 大雨, 中古, 古代, 着物, 人間, 氏名). Where a compound uses a reading I had not met (myō in 名字, shō in 正午, ko in 中古, gen in 人間) or a meaning I had not met (代 as "era" in 古代, when I only knew it as "Charge; fee"), the back gives me the reading and the English, which is all a reading card needs. 梅雨 (tsuyu) cannot be worked out from its kanji, but the card teaches it and its English sets it apart from 雨期. Nothing in the unit is wrong as far as my cards can tell me.

## Strong spots

- Near-synonyms are glossed so I can tell them apart on the back: 友人 "Friend (formal)" next to 友だち "Friend", 知人 "Acquaintance (formal)" next to 知り合い "Acquaintance", 少々 "A little (formal)" next to 少し "A little; a bit", and 正午 "Noon (exactly 12:00)" next to the kanji 午 "Noon" (r-4dfe8f5f2b38, r-faca978c950c, r-523ba26a829c, r-697b80373ae8).
- 梅雨 "Rainy season (in Japan, early summer)" against 雨期 "Rainy season" (r-7d72598db4ca, r-68f92a275c74): the qualifier tells me why there are two words.
- The kanji family groupings land well even shuffled, because every compound reads from parts I know.
