## Findings

**wrong-content**

- `o-prefix` (chapter-1): the note says お is "used almost entirely for things belonging to the
  person you're talking to (or a third person), not your own things". The book only says お is added
  to くに to mean the listener's country. The general claim is false (お is widely used on words
  regardless of whose they are), and the card's own hint names みやげ, whose usual form おみやげ is
  exactly that kind of word.

**untaught-prerequisite**

- `o-prefix` (chapter-1): the Production-front hint cites なまえ ("name") and みやげ ("souvenir"),
  neither taught by this point; the book's example for this chapter is くに.
- `irl-l1-34` (chapter-1-extras): the note writes 先生 in kanji in a kana-only deck.

**book-mismatch**

- `irl-l1-28` (chapter-1-extras): こちらはイギリスのライアンです introduces someone with こちらは but no
  さん. The book glosses こちら as "this person" and every こちらは introduction it prints puts さん on
  the name; its NOTES say さん is dropped only on your own name.
- `irl-l1-28` (chapter-1-extras): English "from England" for イギリス, which the book glosses as
  "United Kingdom".
- `irl-l1-27` (chapter-1-extras): English "Is Ryan English?" for イギリスじん, which the book (and
  this deck's igirisujin card) render as "British".

**book-gap** (partial explanations, polish)

- `yoroshiku-onegaishimasu` (chapter-1): the book's second use (taking leave after asking a favour)
  and its gloss of よろしく as "well" are not on any card.
- `san-suffix-ch14` (chapter-1): the book's points that さん is used regardless of gender and with
  either last or given name are not on any card.

**note-unverifiable**

- `td-intro-tanaka-short` (chapter-1-extras): the note says Tanaka "drops the company here because
  Smith had already named it", a reason the book does not give.

**other**

- `irl-l1-26`, `irl-l1-27` (chapter-1-extras): the targets end in a full-width ？ after か. The book
  never writes ？ after a か question, and no other card in this chapter does.

Not reported: preflight's romaji-style, inline-romaji, near-siblings ("◇, what country are you
from?"), production-length, note-claims and taught-never-used INFO lines for these two units, and
its semantic-dedup exclusion count.
