English is the book's English throughout, the two "said when" glosses for いただきます and
ごちそうさま are kept verbatim, and the scenes on the shared-target cards quote the book's own
situations.

## Findings

### book-mismatch

- **konnichiwa** (should-fix): the note says "A neutral daytime greeting"; the book says "A rather
  informal greeting used from about 10:00 A.M. until sundown."
- **sayounara** (should-fix): the hint says it is "not for friends, family or colleagues you will
  see again soon" and the note calls it "fairly formal". The book's panel shows a teacher saying it
  to schoolchildren, people who meet every day, and the book gives the formal good-bye separately as
  しつれいします ("Said on formal occasions"). The ja-mata note repeats the framing ("for a more
  formal parting use さようなら").
- **iie-not-at-all** (should-fix): the note ends "not a direct answer to a yes/no question". Read
  alone this says いいえ does not answer yes/no questions, which the book contradicts in Lesson 1
  ("いいえ is virtually the same as 'no.'").

### note-unverifiable

- **chotto-matte-kudasai** (polish): the note explains まって as "the て-form of まつ" and says
  おねがいします is "slightly softer" than ください. This chapter teaches the phrase whole; the
  て-form and まつ are not taught until much later, and the softness comparison is not something the
  book says. The mou-ichido-onegaishimasu note repeats the comparison.

Not reported: preflight's romaji terminal-punctuation INFO lines and its three inline-romaji lines
for this unit. (Notes on itadakimasu, gochisousama-deshita, otsukaresama-deshita and
mou-ichido-onegaishimasu also spell long vowels as "ou" where the cards use macrons; preflight's
inline-romaji check does not list them, but it is a style matter, not fidelity.)
