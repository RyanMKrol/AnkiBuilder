# Lesson 1: Meeting: Nice to Meet You (learner)

This was a good first real lesson to study. Coming from 18 set phrases, I got the whole X は Y です
frame, か for questions, の for "of", and a stack of countries with their じん nationalities, and
almost every card stood on its own in a shuffle. The extras unit does the heavy lifting well: it
drills the same frame with different names, separates country from nationality (インドです against
インドじんです), and uses hints to keep near-identical English apart. My problems were small. One card
breaks the さん rule the rest of the chapter works hard to teach. A handful of notes quote the book
("in the source", "NOTES 2", "the Key Sentence") or overstate a rule. The deck's own "in real life"
cards (the irl-* ids) spell romaji and English differently from everything around them.

## Strong spots

- **Country versus nationality** is taught by contrast, not just stated: w2-indo-desu,
  w2-oosutoraria-desu, w2-chuugoku-desu, w2-doitsu-desu, sp1-amerika-desu, each with a note pointing
  at the じん form. w2-watashi-no-kuni-nihon adds the no-お-on-your-own-country point neatly.
- **さん on others, never on yourself** is drilled from both sides: w2-harisu-desu against
  w2-harisu-san-desu, w2-kochira-hoffman, td-intro-tanaka-short.
- **Hints that separate same-English cards**: w2-nihonjin-q-bare ("the short form, with the topic
  left out") against w2-anata-wa-nihonjin-q ("with あなた spelled out"); iie's "answering a yes-or-no
  question" keeps it apart from chapter-0's いいえ "Not at all"; w2-okuni-dochira-bare against the
  name-first versions.
- **Notes that explain structure**: w2-harris-o-onegaishimasu (を against は),
  w2-uketsuke-o-onegaishimasu, w2-kochira-harris (why there is no の inside オーストラリアたいしかん),
  w2-raja-wa-toukyou-daigaku, w2-sp2-sasaki-intro (the person spoken to versus the person spoken
  about), w2-harris-australian-q (no inversion).
- yoroshiku-onegaishimasu, kochirakoso and td-kochirakoso-yoroshiku form a clear exchange.

## Weak spots

**wrong-content**

- chapter-1-extras/irl-l1-28 (should-fix): こちらはイギリスのライアンです introduces someone else
  with no さん, against w2-kochira-hoffman's "さん stays on because you are naming somebody else".
  Also glosses イギリス as "England" where the deck taught "United Kingdom".
- chapter-1-extras/irl-l1-26, irl-l1-27 (polish): the English drops "-san" that the target has
  ("Is Jack French?", "Is Ryan English?"), and irl-l1-27 says "English" for イギリスじん, taught as
  "British".

**untaught-prerequisite**

- chapter-1/dochira: "Where (polite word for どこ)". どこ is on no card.

**missing-info**

- chapter-1/onegaishimasu-pattern-ch14: needs を and doesn't say what it is. The prompt "Please
  (I'd like to see Tanaka-san)" is awkward.
- chapter-1-extras/notes-iie-chigaimasu: ちがいます is never glossed on its own.

**note-unverifiable**

- chapter-1/o-prefix: "used almost entirely for things belonging to the person you're talking to",
  while the hint's own example is みやげ "souvenir". The hint uses two untaught words when おくに is
  right there.
- chapter-1-extras/w2-harisu-san-desu: ハリスです "can only mean 'I am Harris'", which irl-l1-28
  contradicts.

**other**

- Notes that cite the book: chapter-1/san-suffix-ch14 and desu-suffix ("in the source"),
  chapter-1-extras/td-hai-sou-desu ("NOTES 2"), w2-watashi-wa-igirisujin ("the Key Sentence").
- Romaji out of step with the vocabulary cards: chapter-1/fib-c1-hoffman-not-british
  ("doitsu jin"), chapter-1-extras/irl-l1-19 ("furansu jin"), irl-l1-21 ("kankoku jin"), irl-l1-26,
  irl-l1-27 ("igirisu jin"), fib-hoffman-engineer-a ("iie" where every other card has "īe").
- chapter-1-extras/w2-harris-no-okuni-q: the note says every other の in the lesson joins a company
  to a person, but ラジャさんのおくに, わたしのくに and イギリスのライアン are all in this unit.
- chapter-1-extras/irl-l1-34: the note writes 先生 in kanji, which I can't read.
- chapter-1-extras/td-intro-tanaka-short: the note leans on a scene ("Smith had already named it")
  the card never shows.
- chapter-1-extras/fib-watashi-wa-enjinia-desu: category "School & Education" on a job sentence.

Not re-reported: preflight's romaji-style flags, the sou-desu "answerable alone" flag, the long
Production fronts, the o-prefix note-claims flag, the note-romaji mismatches, and the three
"◇, what country are you from?" cards.
